1. Role Overview & Access

Your Role: As a Fault Reporter, you're the first line of communication for electrical system issues, responsible for accurate fault reporting and initial customer liaison.

Key Responsibilities:

  • Create accurate and detailed fault reports
  • Assign appropriate priority levels
  • Communicate with customers about fault status
  • Monitor fault location progress
  • Coordinate with depot management
  • Maintain records of customer interactions

System Access Permissions:

  • ✅ Create new fault reports
  • ✅ View all fault reports you've created
  • ✅ Update fault details and customer information
  • ✅ Track fault location progress
  • ✅ Communicate with field teams
  • ✅ Generate customer status reports
  • ❌ Cannot assign teams to faults
  • ❌ Cannot modify team assignments
  • ❌ Cannot access depot operations

Depot Restrictions:

Important: You can only create fault reports for your assigned depot(s). This ensures proper local coordination and resource allocation.

2. Creating Fault Reports

Accessing the Fault Reporting Form:

Step 1: Log in to the Fault Locator System
Step 2: Navigate to
Create New Fault Report
Step 3: The system displays the fault reporting form

Required Information Fields:

Customer Information:
  • Customer name and contact details
  • Service address and location
  • Customer priority status (if applicable)
  • Alternative contact information
Fault Details:
  • Description of the electrical issue
  • When the fault occurred
  • Symptoms observed by customer
  • Any safety concerns reported
Technical Information:
  • Estimated voltage level affected
  • Number of customers affected
  • Possible backfeed availability
  • Asset information (if known)
Location Details:
  • Precise fault location
  • Access requirements
  • Landmark references
  • GPS coordinates (if available)

Step-by-Step Reporting Process:

Step 1: Gather Information
  • Listen carefully to customer description
  • Ask clarifying questions about symptoms
  • Confirm location and access details
  • Note any safety concerns
Step 2: Assess Priority Level
  • Consider customer impact
  • Evaluate safety implications
  • Check for VIP customer status
  • Determine urgency based on guidelines
Step 3: Complete Form
  • Fill all required fields completely
  • Use clear, descriptive language
  • Include relevant technical details
  • Add any special instructions
Step 4: Review and Submit
  • Double-check all information for accuracy
  • Verify priority level is appropriate
  • Confirm depot assignment is correct
  • Submit fault report

3. Understanding Priority Levels

Priority Assignment Guidelines:

VVIP (Very Very Important Person) Priority:
  • Criteria: Hospitals, emergency services, critical infrastructure
  • Response: Immediate - highest priority
  • Examples: Hospital power failure, police station outage
  • Action: Notify depot foreperson immediately after reporting
Critical Priority (Level 4):
  • Criteria: High voltage faults, major customer impact, safety hazards
  • Response: Within 2 hours
  • Examples: 66kV line fault, multiple customer outage, safety concern
High Priority (Level 3):
  • Criteria: Significant customer impact, medium voltage issues
  • Response: Within 4 hours
  • Examples: 33kV fault, commercial customer outage, multiple residences
Medium Priority (Level 2):
  • Criteria: Moderate impact, low voltage faults
  • Response: Within 8 hours
  • Examples: Single customer outage, intermittent supply issues
Low Priority (Level 1):
  • Criteria: Minor issues, non-urgent matters
  • Response: Next business day
  • Examples: Routine maintenance requests, minor voltage variations

Priority Decision Factors:

Consider These Factors:
  • Customer Impact: How many customers affected?
  • Voltage Level: Higher voltage = higher priority
  • Safety Risk: Any immediate danger?
  • Customer Type: Essential services get priority
  • Time of Day: Business hours vs. after hours
  • Weather Conditions: May affect response urgency
When in Doubt: If unsure about priority level, err on the side of caution and assign a higher priority. The depot foreperson can adjust if necessary.

4. Tracking Fault Status

Fault Status Overview:

Status Progression:
  1. Reported: Fault report created and submitted
  2. Assigned: Team assigned to locate fault
  3. In Progress: Team actively working on fault location
  4. Located: Fault found and position confirmed
  5. Resolved: Fault cleared and customers restored

Monitoring Your Reports:

Viewing Active Faults:
  1. Navigate to
    My Fault Reports
  2. System displays all your active reports
  3. Status updates show current progress
  4. Click on any fault for detailed information

Understanding Status Updates:

Team Started Work:
  • Team has begun fault location activities
  • Expected completion time may be provided
  • Customer can be informed work is in progress
Fault Located:
  • Fault position has been determined
  • Repair or clearance work can begin
  • Customer restoration may be imminent
Customer Restored:
  • Electrical supply has been restored
  • Fault report can be closed
  • Customer should be notified of restoration

Automated Notifications:

You'll Receive Updates When:
  • Team is assigned to your fault report
  • Field work begins
  • Fault is successfully located
  • Customer supply is restored
  • Additional information is needed

6. Reporting Guidelines

Information Quality Standards:

Complete Information:
  • All required fields must be completed
  • Descriptions should be clear and detailed
  • Contact information must be accurate
  • Location details should be precise
Accurate Descriptions:
  • Use customer's exact words for symptoms
  • Avoid technical jargon unless confirmed
  • Include relevant background information
  • Note any previous related issues

Common Reporting Mistakes to Avoid:

Avoid These Errors:
  • Incomplete customer contact information
  • Vague fault descriptions
  • Incorrect priority assignment
  • Missing location details
  • Failing to note safety concerns
  • Not recording customer preferences

Best Practices:

Excellence Standards:
  • Double-check all information before submitting
  • Use clear, professional language
  • Include all relevant details
  • Follow up on critical faults
  • Maintain accurate records
  • Document all customer interactions

Special Situations:

Multiple Customer Reports:
  • Check for existing reports in the same area
  • Add customer details to existing fault if appropriate
  • Create new report if fault is genuinely separate
  • Coordinate with depot to avoid duplication
Emergency Situations:
  • Create fault report immediately
  • Use VVIP or Critical priority as appropriate
  • Contact depot foreperson by phone
  • Follow emergency reporting procedures

8. Troubleshooting

Common System Issues:

Issue: Cannot create fault report for specific depot
Solution:
  • Verify you have permissions for that depot
  • Check if depot is correctly selected
  • Contact system administrator if access needed
  • Use alternative depot if customer is on boundary
Issue: Form validation errors prevent submission
Solution:
  • Check all required fields are completed
  • Verify contact information format
  • Ensure priority level is selected
  • Review location details for completeness
Issue: System performance is slow
Solution:
  • Check your internet connection
  • Close unnecessary browser tabs
  • Clear browser cache if needed
  • Try refreshing the page
Issue: Cannot track fault status updates
Solution:
  • Verify fault report was successfully submitted
  • Check "My Fault Reports" section
  • Ensure browser allows system notifications
  • Contact depot for status if system shows no updates

Customer Communication Issues:

Issue: Customer provides conflicting information
Solution:
  • Ask clarifying questions to resolve conflicts
  • Document all versions in fault notes
  • Prioritize safety-related information
  • Follow up with customer for verification
Issue: Unsure about priority level assignment
Solution:
  • Consult priority guidelines in this manual
  • Contact depot foreperson for guidance
  • Err on side of caution with higher priority
  • Document reasoning in fault notes
